## 1. Purpose

This document defines dispatch eligibility for testing. Actual licensing requirements vary by work type and jurisdiction; this document must not be treated as proof that any person is legally qualified.

## 2. In-house maintenance may assess or perform

Subject to training, site authorization, and safe conditions, in-house maintenance may:
- inspect and document nonhazardous visible damage;
- replace ordinary interior light bulbs where safely accessible and permitted;
- adjust cabinet hardware, door handles, and nonsecurity hardware;
- perform basic fixture checks that do not require opening energized equipment;
- install approved replacement filters;
- perform visual checks and document appliance symptoms;
- place a safe area restriction or request a vendor when a condition exceeds staff authorization.

These permissions do not authorize work on energized wiring, gas systems, fire protection systems, structural elements, refrigerant circuits, or any task requiring a credential that the assigned person does not hold.

## 3. Licensed or approved specialist dispatch

Use an appropriately qualified and property-approved specialist for:
- electrical panel, circuit, outlet, or wiring repair beyond a permitted basic visual check;
- gas piping, gas appliance connections, or suspected gas-system defects;
- refrigerant handling or sealed HVAC-system repair;
- fire alarm, sprinkler, or other life-safety system repair;
- suspected structural damage, unstable balconies, or major building-envelope failure;
- sewage contamination remediation or hazardous-material conditions;
- pest treatment involving restricted chemicals or conditions outside the approved pest vendor's scope.

The system must not infer a vendor's license, insurance, availability, or approval from a trade label alone. These details must come from a verified vendor record.

## 4. Dispatch selection

Choose the trade based on observed symptoms and policy eligibility. A report may require multiple trades. When root cause is uncertain, select “human triage required” rather than asserting a definitive diagnosis.

Examples:
- “Kitchen faucet drips at the handle” → plumbing/general maintenance assessment, subject to site rules.
- “Outlet sparks when a plug is inserted” → safety escalation and qualified electrician; do not advise resident troubleshooting.
- “AC is warm and the outdoor unit makes a loud noise” → HVAC review; no claim that refrigerant is low without verified diagnosis.
- “Ceiling is sagging and wet” → immediate safety/property-damage escalation; do not route as cosmetic drywall repair.

## 5. Decisions not authorized

No technician or AI assessment under this policy may independently authorize lease penalties, tenant billing, rent adjustments, insurance claims, code-compliance certifications, or permanent repairs outside documented scope. Obtain separate verified approval.
