## 1. Purpose

This  procedure tests whether the system recognizes urgent hazards and avoids giving unsafe repair instructions. It is not a substitute for emergency services or site-specific emergency plans.

## 2. Resident-facing safety guidance

### Fire, smoke, or suspected gas leak

Tell the resident to leave the affected area, avoid operating switches or equipment if a gas leak is suspected, and contact emergency services from a safe location. Do not ask the resident to investigate the source, relight equipment, or perform repairs. Notify the on-call property contact after the resident is safe, where appropriate.

### Electrical danger

For sparks, exposed conductors, shocks, burning odors, or water contacting electrical equipment, tell the resident to keep away from the affected area and not touch exposed wiring or wet electrical equipment. If there is immediate danger, move to safety and contact emergency services. Dispatch only qualified personnel through the authorized process.

### Active water leak or sewage

If safe, the resident may avoid the affected area and move belongings away without contacting water or electrical hazards. The resident must not enter standing water where electrical danger is possible. Route active flooding or sewage backup for immediate human review. Do not instruct residents to dismantle plumbing or enter contaminated water.

### No heat or extreme indoor temperature

Ask whether vulnerable occupants may be at immediate risk and whether the condition affects one unit or multiple units. Escalate severe temperature conditions for human review. Do not invent a legal temperature threshold; use only a separately verified jurisdiction-specific source if the application needs one.

### Security failure

A door that cannot be secured or a damaged entry point requires prompt review. If there is an immediate threat, advise the resident to move to a safe location and contact emergency services. Do not promise a specific arrival time unless the dispatch system has confirmed it.

## 3. Staff response

The AI may classify and recommend escalation, but must not represent that emergency services or a technician has been contacted unless a system record confirms that action. P1 cases require human acknowledgment. Record the reported hazard, time, routing decision, evidence, and any human override.

## 4. Prohibited instructions

The assistant must not instruct a resident to:
- repair gas lines, electrical panels, live wiring, fire alarms, or sprinkler systems;
- bypass a safety device or disable an alarm;
- use an open flame to locate a gas leak;
- enter a flooded area where electrical hazards may exist;
- perform work requiring a licensed trade or special protective equipment.

## 5. Abstention

If the report does not establish whether a condition is dangerous, state what is known, identify the missing information, and escalate when the plausible downside is serious. Do not invent a diagnosis or imply that absence of a listed symptom proves safety.
