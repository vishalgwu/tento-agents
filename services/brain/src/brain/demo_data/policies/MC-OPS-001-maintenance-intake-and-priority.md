## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy defines a demonstration workflow for recording, prioritizing, and routing maintenance reports at the  property. It is a software test fixture only. It does not define legal response deadlines or replace emergency services, local codes, a lease, or an approved property policy.

## 2. Intake requirements

Every request should capture, when available: property and unit identifier; reporter's authorized relationship to the unit; issue category; location within the unit or common area; when the issue began; whether it is active or worsening; affected utilities or fixtures; visible damage; photos voluntarily supplied by the resident; access constraints; and a safe callback method.

The system must not require a resident to diagnose the underlying cause. A report such as “water is coming through the ceiling” is sufficient to begin triage.

## 3. Demonstration priority classes

### P1 — Immediate safety or active property-damage concern

Examples include active flooding or a burst pipe; sewage backup; smoke, fire, or suspected gas leak; sparking, exposed live wiring, or burning electrical odor; a failed exterior door that leaves a unit unable to secure; a fallen or unstable structural component; or loss of heat during a severe cold-weather condition when people may be at risk.

**Routing:** Flag for immediate human review and on-call dispatch. For fire, suspected gas leak, immediate threat to life, or other imminent danger, instruct the resident to move to safety and contact the appropriate emergency service. Do not wait for a portal response.

### P2 — Urgent service interruption or worsening defect

Examples include a toilet that cannot be used when no alternative is available; a significant but contained leak; a refrigerator failure affecting medication storage; loss of hot water affecting the unit; or a lock/entry issue that is concerning but not an immediate threat.

**Routing:** Queue for same-day review during staffed operations. If new facts indicate imminent danger or active damage, reclassify as P1.

### P3 — Routine repair

Examples include a dripping tap, a slow drain that remains usable, a broken cabinet hinge, a damaged blind, a noncritical light fixture, or an appliance issue that does not create an immediate hazard.

**Routing:** Queue for ordinary scheduling. Demonstration target: initial review within two business days. This is an illustrative internal target, not a guarantee or legal deadline.

### P4 — Planned, cosmetic, or preventive work

Examples include paint touch-up, a nonurgent adjustment, or a scheduled preventive inspection.

**Routing:** Bundle or schedule according to staffing and access. The system should communicate that timing depends on scope, parts, access, and vendor availability.

## 4. Classification rules

- Classify based on reported symptoms and potential consequences, not guessed root cause.
- When information is insufficient to distinguish P1 from a lower priority, preserve the uncertainty and request immediate human review if a safety risk is plausible.
- A resident's statement that a condition is “not an emergency” does not override evidence of an imminent hazard.
- Never downgrade an active hazard solely to meet a service target.
- Record the reason for any human priority override.

## 5. Out-of-scope decisions

This policy does not authorize rent credits, lease enforcement, resident chargebacks, habitability determinations, legal conclusions, or promises of repair completion. Those require separate verified authority and human review.
